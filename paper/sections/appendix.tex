\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection*{\Cref{lem:props}}
Write $u = DF\,\xi$ and $v = D\xi\,F$ as elements of $\Ltwo{\nu}$. Then
$\defect^2 = \|u-v\|^2/(\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2)$. The parallelogram-type inequality
$\|u-v\|^2 \le 2(\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2)$ gives $\defect \le \sqrt 2$, with equality
iff $u = -v$; $\defect = 0$ iff $u = v$ in $\Ltwo{\nu}$, i.e.\ $[\xi,F]=0$
$\nu$-almost everywhere, and since both are polynomials this holds on the
support of $\nu$; that this is equivalent to the flow of $\xi$ mapping solutions
to solutions is standard \citep[Ch.~9]{lee2013smooth}. Under
$\xi \mapsto c\xi$ both $u$ and $v$ scale by $c$; under
$F \mapsto c'F$ both scale by $c'$; the ratio is invariant in each case, hence
also under any invertible linear reparameterisation of the candidate class,
because $\defect$ depends on $\theta$ only through the field $\xi_\theta$.
For $x = cQz$ with $Q$ orthogonal, the pullbacks are
$\tilde F(z) = c^{-1}Q^{\!\top}F(cQz)$ and likewise for $\xi$, so
$\tilde u = c^{-1}Q^{\!\top}u \circ (cQ)$ and similarly for $v$; the factor
$c^{-1}Q^{\!\top}$ is a scalar multiple of an isometry and cancels in the
ratio once $\nu$ is transported. \qed

\subsection*{\Cref{lem:bilinear}}
$DF_\beta$ and $F_\beta$ are linear in $\beta$, and $\xi_\theta$ and
$D\xi_\theta$ are linear in $\theta$, so each of $DF_\beta\,\xi_\theta$ and
$D\xi_\theta F_\beta$ is a polynomial vector field whose coefficient vector is
bilinear in $(\theta,\beta)$. Collecting coefficients over the monomial basis of
the residual space and multiplying by the symmetric square root of the monomial
Gram matrix of $\nu$ (which exists because the Gram matrix is positive
semi-definite) turns the $\Ltwo{\nu}$ norms into Euclidean norms, giving
$M^{(1)}_\theta$ and $M^{(2)}_\theta$; $L_\theta$ is their difference by
\eqref{eq:bracket}. Assembling $C(\beta) = A(\beta)^{\!\top}A(\beta)$ with
$A(\beta)_{\cdot p} = L_{e_p}\beta$ and $D(\beta)$ analogously,
$\defect^2 = \theta^{\!\top}C\theta / \theta^{\!\top}D\theta$, whose minimum
over $\theta \ne 0$ is the smallest generalised eigenvalue of $(C,D)$ whenever
$D \succ 0$. \qed

\subsection*{\Cref{prop:simultaneous}}
On the event $E = \{\beta^\star \in \mathcal B\}$ we have, for every $\theta$,
$\|L_\theta\beta^\star\| \le \sup_{\beta \in \mathcal B}\|L_\theta\beta\|$ and
$\|M^{(j)}_\theta\beta^\star\| \ge \inf_{\beta\in\mathcal B}
\|M^{(j)}_\theta\beta\|$ for $j = 1,2$, so
$\defect_\nu(\xi_\theta;F_{\beta^\star}) \le U(\theta)$; the lower bound is
symmetric. Since $\Prob(E) \ge 1-\alpha$ and $E$ does not depend on $\theta$,
the statement holds simultaneously. For \eqref{eq:fwer}: on $E$, any $\xi$ with
$U(\xi) \le \delta$ has $\defect_\nu(\xi;F_{\beta^\star}) \le \delta$,
whatever rule produced it; so the bad event is contained in $E^c$. \qed

\subsection*{\Cref{prop:closedform}}
Writing $\mathcal B = \{\bhat + \hat\Sigma^{1/2}u : \|u\|\le R\}$, each
extremum is $\max$ or $\min$ of $\|a + Bu\|$ over $\|u\| \le R$ with
$a = L_\theta\bhat$ and $B = L_\theta\hat\Sigma^{1/2}$. Squaring, this is the
optimisation of $u^{\!\top}Qu + 2c^{\!\top}u$ over a ball with $Q = B^{\!\top}B
\succeq 0$ and $c = B^{\!\top}a$: the trust-region subproblem, whose solutions
satisfy $(Q \mp \mu I)u = \mp c$ with the multiplier fixed by the secular
equation $\sum_i \tilde c_i^2/(\mu \mp \lambda_i)^2 = R^2$ in the eigenbasis of
$Q$, monotone in $\mu$ and solvable by bisection; the hard case ($c$ orthogonal
to the extreme eigenspace) is handled by adding a component in that eigenspace
\citep{gander1989constrained,more1983computing,conn2000trust}. For the exactness
of the region: in model \eqref{eq:model}, $\bhat - \beta^\star \sim
N(0, \sigma^2 \Sigma_0)$ with $\Sigma_0 = I_n \otimes (\Phi^{\!\top}\Phi)^{-1}$
and $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{\mathrm{dof}}$ independent of $\bhat$,
so $\|\hat\Sigma^{-1/2}(\bhat-\beta^\star)\|^2/Q$, with
$\hat\Sigma = \hat\sigma^2\Sigma_0$, is exactly $F_{Q,\mathrm{dof}}$
distributed. \qed

\subsection*{\Cref{prop:lecam}}
Fix $\xi$, and let $M_\xi$ stack $M^{(1)}_\xi$ and $M^{(2)}_\xi$. Choose
$\Delta$ attaining $s_{\max}(\xi)$, normalised so
$\|F_\Delta\|_{\Ltwo{\mu_N}} = 1$ where $\mu_N$ is the empirical design measure,
and set $\beta^t = \beta^0 + t\Delta$. Since $L_\xi \beta^0 = 0$,
$\|L_\xi\beta^t\| = t\,s_{\max}$ and
$\|M_\xi \beta^t\| \le \|M_\xi\beta^0\| + t\|M_\xi\Delta\|$, so
$\defect_\nu(\xi;F_{\beta^t}) \ge \delta$ for
$t \ge t_\delta = \delta\|M_\xi\beta^0\|/(s_{\max}-\delta\|M_\xi\Delta\|)$.
The Kullback--Leibler divergence between the two data distributions is
$\mathrm{KL} = \tfrac{N t^2}{2\sigma^2}\|F_\Delta\|^2_{\Ltwo{\mu_N}}
= Nt^2/(2\sigma^2)$, so by Pinsker's inequality the total variation distance is
at most $t\sqrt N/(2\sigma)$. For any test $\varphi$,
$\Prob_{\beta^0}(\varphi=1) \le \Prob_{\beta^{t}}(\varphi=1) +
\mathrm{TV} \le \alpha + t\sqrt N/(2\sigma)$ for any $t$ slightly above
$t_\delta$; taking $t \downarrow t_\delta$ and expanding
$t_\delta = \delta\|M_\xi\beta^0\|/s_{\max} + O(\delta^2)$ gives the claim.
The argument is the standard two-point (Le~Cam) method
\citep[Ch.~2]{tsybakov2009introduction}. \qed

\subsection*{\Cref{prop:kappa}}
Both $\|r\|^2_{\Ltwo{\nu}}$ and $\|r\|^2_{\Ltwo{\mu}}$ are quadratic forms in
the coefficient vector $c$ of $r \in \mathcal R$, equal to $c^{\!\top}G_\nu c$
and $c^{\!\top}G_\mu c$; their ratio is a generalised Rayleigh quotient whose
maximum is $\lambda_{\max}(G_\mu^{-1/2}G_\nu G_\mu^{-1/2})$, attained at the
corresponding eigenvector, which is a legitimate element of $\mathcal R$. The
defect statement follows by applying the bound to the numerator and its reverse
to each denominator block. If $G_\mu$ is singular with null vector $c \ne 0$
then $\|r_c\|_{\Ltwo{\mu}} = 0$ while $\|r_c\|_{\Ltwo{\nu}}>0$ (as $G_\nu \succ
0$ for a measure with a density on an open set), so $\kappa = \infty$;
conversely $\kappa = \infty$ forces $G_\mu$ to be singular. \qed

\section{Construction of the tensors}
\label{app:construction}

Represent a polynomial vector field on $\R^n$ by an $n \times m$ array of
coefficients over the monomials of total degree at most $D$. Multiplication and
differentiation of monomials are index tables computed once. The bracket
\eqref{eq:bracket} of two such fields is then exact integer/rational linear
algebra. Fixing the candidate basis $\{\zeta_p\}$ and the model basis
$\{x^a e_i\}$ and computing $[\zeta_p, x^a e_i]$ for every pair gives a
three-index tensor $T \in \R^{K \times P \times Q}$ with
$\mathrm{coef}([\xi_\theta,F_\beta]) = T \cdot \theta \cdot \beta$, and likewise
$T^{(1)}, T^{(2)}$ for the two individual terms, with $T = T^{(1)}-T^{(2)}$.
Multiplying the first index by $I_n \otimes g_\nu^{1/2}$, where $g_\nu$ is the
monomial Gram matrix of $\nu$, converts Euclidean norms of contracted vectors
into $\Ltwo{\nu}$ norms of the corresponding fields. Then
$L_\theta = T_w \cdot \theta$ and $A(\beta) = T_w \cdot \beta$.

$g_\nu$ is available in closed form for the uniform measure on a box, the
Gaussian, and the uniform measure on a ball or annulus (the last two by
separating radial and angular moments), so no quadrature error enters. For the
\emph{data} measure we substitute the empirical Gram matrix of the same monomial
basis; the difference between the two is exactly what \Cref{prop:kappa}
measures.

For the refutation bound we need
$\min_{\theta \ne 0}\defect_\nu(\xi_\theta;F_{\beta^\star})$ from below.
On the event $\{\beta^\star \in \mathcal B\}$,
$\sigma_{\min}(A(\beta^\star)) \ge \sigma_{\min}(A(\bhat)) -
\sup_{\beta \in \mathcal B}\|A(\beta - \bhat)\|_F$ and
$\sigma_{\max}(M(\beta^\star)) \le \sigma_{\max}(M(\bhat)) +
\sup_{\beta \in \mathcal B}\|M(\beta-\bhat)\|_F$, and each supremum is again a
norm of a linear image of $\beta$, hence another trust-region maximum. The
ratio of the two bounds is the reported lower bound. The subspace certificate
is the same construction with $A$ and $M$ restricted to the subspace.

\section{Baselines}
\label{app:baselines}

\paragraph{Fixed threshold.} Declare the span of all generalised eigenvectors of
$(C(\bhat), D(\bhat))$ whose plug-in defect is at most $\tau$. Sweeping $\tau$
traces the whole family of threshold rules, including the choice $\tau = \delta$
that matches our tolerance.

\paragraph{Eigengap.} Declare $d$ maximising the relative gap
$\defect_{(d+1)}/\defect_{(d)}$ over $d \ge 1$. This rule cannot express ``no
symmetry'', which is one reason its error rate is high; we report it because it
is widely used, and we also report the threshold family, which can.

\paragraph{Weyl perturbation certificate.} Bound
$\lambda_k(C(\beta^\star)) \le \lambda_k(C(\bhat)) + \|C(\beta^\star)-C(\bhat)\|$
with the perturbation bounded by
$2\|A(\bhat)\|\,\|\Delta A\|_F + \|\Delta A\|_F^2$ and $\|\Delta A\|_F$
maximised over the same confidence ellipsoid, then divide by a lower bound on
$\lambda_{\min}(D)$. This is the natural singular-value perturbation answer
\citep{bhatia1997matrix,davis1970rotation,yu2015useful}, the same route one would
take with matrix concentration \citep{tropp2012user} if the Gram matrix were
estimated by averaging over samples rather than integrated exactly; it is valid,
and it is
loose because it spends one global perturbation budget on every direction at
once instead of bounding the ratio direction by direction.

\paragraph{Rank test.} For each $d$, test $H_0$: the $d$ smallest plug-in
directions span an exact nullspace, using the squared $\Ltwo{\nu}$ norm of the
restricted bracket referred to a Welch--Satterthwaite approximation of its null
distribution (a weighted sum of $\chi^2_1$ variables), and report the largest
$d$ not rejected. This is a Wald-type rank test in the spirit of
\citet{robin2000tests} and \citet{kleibergen2006generalized}.

\paragraph{Split significance test.} Select the smallest-defect direction on one
half of the data; on the other half test $H_0$: that direction is an exact
symmetry, by the same Wald statistic; declare a symmetry when the test fails to
reject. The selection is honest and the test is valid; the declaration is not.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

All experiments are deterministic given the seeds recorded in the code. The
package, the experiment scripts, the figure scripts and the raw result files are
released together. Every table body in the manuscript is written by
\texttt{paper/make\_tables.py} directly from those files rather than
transcribed, and every number that appears in the running text is asserted
against them by \texttt{paper/check\_numbers.py}, which verifies $31$ claims and
fails if any has drifted.

\texttt{results/compute\_log.json} records the CPU and wall time of the most
recent run of each experiment; the recorded total for one complete pass of the
suite is given in the introduction. Wall-clock times in that file are not
meaningful --- the machine was shared with unrelated jobs throughout --- and the
CPU times should be read as the cost of one pass, not as the total cost of the
work, since several experiments were re-run during development.

The correctness of the mathematical core is checked by a test suite that
verifies: the bracket against finite differences; the closed-form monomial
moments of all four target measures against Monte-Carlo integration; the defect
against Monte-Carlo evaluation of its defining integrals; the exact symmetry
algebra of linear systems against the dimension of the commutant computed by an
independent route; the trust-region extrema against brute-force search over the
ball; the Welch--Satterthwaite approximation against simulation; and the
empirical coverage of the certificate against its nominal level.
