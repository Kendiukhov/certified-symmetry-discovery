Dear Dr Li,

Thank you for the opportunity to revise the manuscript previously titled
``Certified Symmetry Discovery: Confidence Sets for Lie-Algebra Generators from
Noisy, Limited Data''. I am submitting the revised version, now titled
\textbf{``Certified symmetry discovery for polynomial and dictionary vector
fields: confidence sets for Lie-algebra generators from noisy vector-field
measurements''}, together
with a point-by-point response to the three reviewers and a separate
Supplementary Information file.

The reviewers' central request, shared by the editorial assessment, was to align
every practical and empirical claim with what the theorem actually guarantees.
The revision does this through new theory, new code and new experiments rather
than through rewording alone. In brief:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item a table listing each guarantee with the assumptions it needs, and a
  separate treatment of a fixed, a data-selected and a misspecified model class,
  with a new result quantifying how large a symmetry violation a lack-of-fit
  test of given power can miss and a new procedure that remains valid when the
  class is chosen from the data, provided the largest class considered
  contains the truth; a new simulation study evaluates both;
\item the certificate is now the supremum of the defect over the confidence
  region, with the infimum as a lower bound, as one reviewer suggested,
  computed with certified numerics;
\item dependence-robust (cluster and Newey--West) regions, with a new study of
  serially correlated errors that reports the coverage of the coefficient region
  itself;
\item an output metric which, when transported with the coordinates, makes the
  defect, and hence the tolerance, invariant under every affine change of
  coordinates, a non-asymptotic resolution bound, exact binomial intervals for
  the worst-setting rates of the calibration table, for the zero
  false-certification rate of the certificate and for selected rates in the
  text, together with a stated bound on the Monte Carlo error of every other
  rate, a recovery metric, a bracket-closure diagnostic, an extension to
  non-polynomial dictionaries, a demonstration that noisy states can
  manufacture false symmetries, and a study showing that, in the systems
  tested, additive derivative noise only rarely makes a system without
  symmetry look symmetric, and then only near the tolerance, although it
  can tilt a real symmetry (the source of the threshold rule's 63\% worst
  case);
\item a second real data set, ocean drifter velocities in which rotation
  invariance is physically expected, analysed as a case study under stated
  assumptions;
\item a redesigned tightness experiment, which also shows that a narrower
  bootstrap bound fails for a generator selected from the data while the
  certificate does not, the correction of an implementation error found while
  revisiting the trajectory study, and a narrower title.
\end{itemize}
All experiments were re-run with the final code, and the numerical results in
the text are checked automatically against the result files. The manuscript
uses the journal's structure: an unstructured abstract of at most 200 words,
Introduction, Results, Discussion and Methods, numbered references, eight
display items, and the supporting material in a separate Supplementary
Information file. The main text (Introduction, Results and Discussion, without
figure legends) runs to about 12,500 words, and the reference list has 67
entries (59 of them cited in the main text, the rest only in Methods), above
the recommended 4,500 words and about 60 references, mainly because of the
analyses requested in review (data-driven class selection, dependence-robust
inference, the tightness and noisy-state studies and the second real data set)
and the limitations that accompany them. Proofs and secondary analyses are
already in the Methods and the Supplementary Information, and I would be glad
to move further material there if you prefer.

\textbf{Declarations.} This manuscript is original, has not been published
previously, and is not under consideration by another journal. I am the sole
author. I have no competing interests to declare. The work involves no human
participants, no animals and no personal data. The implementation, raw results
and scripts are released in the public repository
\url{https://github.com/Kendiukhov/certified-symmetry-discovery}, where the
version used for the revised manuscript will be tagged \texttt{v2.0.0}.

Thank you for considering the revised manuscript.
