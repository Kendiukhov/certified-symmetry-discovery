\subsection{SymCert: certificates for the defect}
\label{sec:method}

The plug-in defect $\defect_\nu(\xi_\theta; F_{\bhat})$ is an estimate. What a
practitioner needs is a statement about the \emph{true} system: an upper bound
on $\defect_\nu(\xi_\theta; F_{\beta^\star})$ that holds with a stated
probability, for whichever generator the analysis happens to select.

\paragraph{The simultaneous certificate.} Let $\mathcal B \subseteq \R^Q$ be a
confidence region for the true coefficients $\beta^\star$ with
$\Prob(\beta^\star \in \mathcal B) \ge 1-\alpha$, constructed in any way. For
every $\theta \neq 0$ define
\begin{equation}
  U(\theta) \;=\; \sup_{\beta \in \mathcal B}\ \defect_\nu(\xi_\theta; F_\beta),
  \qquad
  L(\theta) \;=\; \inf_{\beta \in \mathcal B}\ \defect_\nu(\xi_\theta; F_\beta),
  \label{eq:U}
\end{equation}
with the convention that $U(\theta) = +\infty$ if the denominator of the defect
vanishes for some $\beta \in \mathcal B$ (the generator cannot then be
certified), and that the infimum is taken over the $\beta$ for which the defect
is defined. The lower bound $L(\theta)$ is not to be confused with the matrix
$L_\theta$ of \Cref{lem:bilinear}.

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous validity]
\label{prop:simultaneous}
If $\Prob(\beta^\star \in \mathcal B) \ge 1-\alpha$, then
$\Prob\bigl(\, L(\theta) \le \defect_\nu(\xi_\theta;F_{\beta^\star}) \le
U(\theta) \ \text{ for every } \theta \text{ with a defined defect} \,\bigr) \ge
1-\alpha$. Consequently, for \emph{any} rule, deterministic or data-dependent,
that selects generators and certifies those with $U \le \delta$, and for any
tolerance, fixed in advance or not,
\begin{equation}
  \Prob\bigl(\, \exists\, \theta, \delta:\ U(\theta) \le \delta < \defect_\nu(\xi_\theta;F_{\beta^\star}) \,\bigr) \;\le\; \alpha .
  \label{eq:fwer}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The proof is one line (Methods): the single event $\{\beta^\star \in
\mathcal B\}$ implies the bound for every generator and every tolerance at
once, so the family-wise false-certification guarantee~\eqref{eq:fwer} comes
for free. No multiplicity correction is applied because none is needed, and no
data splitting is required to protect against the selection of $\theta$, the
problem that motivates split-sample and post-selection inference
\citep{berk2013valid,lee2016exact,meinshausen2009pvalues,andrews2024inference}.
The guarantee is exactly as good as the region $\mathcal B$: it inherits
$\mathcal B$'s coverage and nothing more, and it says nothing about choices made
outside the candidate class, such as the model class, the dictionary or the
target domain (\Cref{sec:guarantee}).

\paragraph{Computation.} Take $\mathcal B$ to be an ellipsoid $\{\bhat +
\hat\Sigma^{1/2} u : \|u\| \le R\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Evaluation]
\label{prop:closedform}
(i) Each of $\sup_{\mathcal B}\|L_\theta\beta\|$ and $\inf_{\mathcal
B}\|M_\theta\beta\|$ is the extremum of $\|a + Bu\|$ over the ball $\|u\| \le
R$, a trust-region subproblem, so the \emph{separate bound}
$U_{\mathrm{sep}}(\theta) = \sup_{\mathcal B}\|L_\theta\beta\| / \inf_{\mathcal
B}\|M_\theta\beta\| \ge U(\theta)$ costs two of them (with
$U_{\mathrm{sep}}(\theta) = +\infty$ when $\inf_{\mathcal B}\|M_\theta\beta\| = 0$).
(ii) If $\inf_{\mathcal B}\|M_\theta\beta\| > 0$ (otherwise $U(\theta) = +\infty$
by the convention of~\eqref{eq:U}), then for every level $t \ge 0$,
$U(\theta)^2 \le t$ if and only if
$\min_{\|u\|\le R}\ t\,\|M_\theta \beta(u)\|^2 - \|L_\theta\beta(u)\|^2 \ge 0$,
again a trust-region subproblem, now with an indefinite quadratic; such
problems are solved globally because their Lagrangian dual has no duality gap
\citep{more1983computing,conn2000trust}. A one-dimensional search on $t$
therefore yields $U(\theta)$, and likewise the lower bound $L(\theta)$, to any
prescribed relative tolerance.
(iii) Under model~\eqref{eq:model} with ordinary least squares and an
$N \times (Q/n)$ design matrix $\Phi$ of the dictionary of full column rank,
taking $\hat\Sigma$ the usual covariance estimate and
$R^2 = Q\,\mathrm{F}_{Q,\,\mathrm{dof};\,1-\alpha}$, where
$\mathrm{F}_{Q,\,\mathrm{dof};\,1-\alpha}$ is a quantile of the $F$
distribution and $\mathrm{dof} = nN - Q$, makes $\mathcal B$ an \emph{exact}
$1-\alpha$ confidence region in finite samples, conditional on the design.
\end{proposition}

Every quantity entering $U$ and $L$ is evaluated through a Lagrangian dual
bound rather than at the point where a root finder stops, so the computed $U$
can only err upwards and the computed $L$ only downwards; the root-finding
tolerance (a relative $10^{-7}$ on $U(\theta)$ and $L(\theta)$ themselves, with a further
relative safety margin of $10^{-9}$ for rounding) affects tightness, never
validity (Methods). The bounds are therefore \emph{finite-sample valid}
whenever $\mathcal B$ is an exact region, and the word \emph{exact} is reserved
here for the coverage of $\mathcal B$ and for the trust-region extrema
themselves. The test suite checks the solvers against brute-force search over
the ball.

Optimising the ratio directly, rather than its numerator and denominator
separately, matters little where the bounds are informative: across the $168$
settings of the resolution study the sharp bound is narrower than
$U_{\mathrm{sep}}$ by a median of $2.5\%$, and by up to a third only where both
bounds are large ($N = 100$, noise level $0.3$). The dominant source of
conservatism is not the separation. The gap between $U$ and the lower bound of
\Cref{prop:lecam} grows with the number $Q$ of model coefficients, but nearly
as much for the pointwise bound below, so it is not mainly a cost of
simultaneity (\Cref{sec:resolution}).

\paragraph{A tighter bound for a pre-specified generator.} When $\theta$ is
fixed in advance, or chosen on a held-out split, the ellipsoid can be replaced
by Gaussian concentration of $\|L_\theta(\bhat - \beta^\star)\|$ about its
mean, giving a width driven by an \emph{effective} dimension
$\mathrm{tr}(\Gamma_\theta)/\|\Gamma_\theta\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ rather than by the
full parameter count. Here $\Gamma_\theta = c\,L_\theta \hat\Sigma
L_\theta^{\!\top}$, with $c = \mathrm{dof}/\chi^2_{\mathrm{dof};\,\alpha_\sigma}$
a chi-square upper confidence factor for the noise variance. Concretely,
$\|\Gamma_\theta^{1/2} z\|$ with $z$ standard Gaussian has mean at most
$(\mathrm{tr}\,\Gamma_\theta)^{1/2}$ and is a Lipschitz function of $z$ with
constant $\|\Gamma_\theta\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{1/2}$, so the Gaussian concentration
inequality \citep{borell1975brunn,cirelson1976norms,boucheron2013concentration}
bounds $\|L_\theta(\bhat - \beta^\star)\|$ by $w_L =
(\mathrm{tr}\,\Gamma_\theta)^{1/2} +
(2\|\Gamma_\theta\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(1/\alpha_L))^{1/2}$ with probability at
least $1 - \alpha_L - \alpha_\sigma$. The denominator is bounded in the same
way ($w_M$, with $M_\theta$ in place of $L_\theta$, at level $\alpha_M$), and
the upper bound is $(\|L_\theta\bhat\| + w_L)/(\|M_\theta\bhat\| - w_M)$, or
$+\infty$ if $w_M \ge \|M_\theta\bhat\|$. With $\alpha_L = 0.98\alpha$ and
$\alpha_M = \alpha_\sigma = 0.01\alpha$ its coverage is at least $1-\alpha$
(Methods). We report it for comparison.

\paragraph{Certify.} $\xi_\theta$ is certified as a $\delta$-approximate
symmetry when $U(\theta) \le \delta$. Equivalently, the smallest tolerance the
data support for that generator, which we call its certified tolerance, is
$U(\theta)$ itself.

\paragraph{Refute.} If $\min_{\theta} L(\theta) > \delta$ then, with confidence
$1-\alpha$, the system has \emph{no} $\delta$-approximate symmetry in the
candidate class. A lower bound on this minimum follows from the same region via
smallest and largest singular values (Methods).

\paragraph{The certified plug-in subspace.} Let $V_r$ be spanned by the $r$
generalised eigenvectors of $(C(\bhat), D(\bhat))$ with smallest plug-in defect.
By the Courant--Fischer theorem $V_r$ minimises the largest \emph{plug-in}
defect among all $r$-dimensional subspaces of the candidate class. We report
the largest $r$ such that every generator in $V_r$ is certified, computed by
bounding the largest and smallest singular values of the restricted maps over
$\mathcal B$, and call it the dimension of the certified plug-in subspace. Two
qualifications are part of its meaning. First, it is a lower bound on the
largest dimension of any certifiable subspace: the plug-in subspace minimises
the plug-in defect, not the upper bound, and the nesting may be suboptimal.
Second, it certifies a subspace of approximate generators, not an approximate
Lie algebra. For exact symmetries the bracket of two symmetries is again a
symmetry, but the set of $\delta$-approximate generators need not be a vector
space, nor be closed under the bracket. Closure is a separate, checkable
property: for generators $\xi_a, \xi_b$ of the subspace we measure the part of
$[\xi_a,\xi_b]$ outside the subspace, relative to the size of its two terms,
and, when the candidate class is closed under the bracket, as the affine class
is, certify the defect of each commutator that is not negligible (Methods,
\STclosure). $V_r$ is data-dependent, which is why
\Cref{prop:simultaneous} is stated for all $\theta$ at once.

\paragraph{Choosing the tolerance after seeing the data.} Because
\eqref{eq:fwer} holds uniformly in $\delta$, the whole curve $\delta \mapsto
\{\theta : U(\theta) \le \delta\}$ is valid simultaneously, and reporting $U$
for every generator of interest is always safe. A tolerance chosen after
inspecting that curve does not invalidate the claim
$\defect_\nu \le \delta$; what it gives up is the scientific meaning of a
tolerance fixed in advance, so a post hoc $\delta$ should be reported as such,
together with $U$.
